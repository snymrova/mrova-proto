01 — Maybe

Most of the men I work with say something like this in the first session.

i.

“I’m lying awake at 3am running through tomorrow’s meeting. Again.”

ii.

“I keep snapping at my wife. I don’t even know why.”

iii.

“Functioning fine on the outside. Exhausted on the inside.”

iv.

“Six months after the accident and I’m still not sleeping right.”

If any of that sounds familiar, you’re in the right place.

02 — How I Work

Therapy that gets to the point.

i.

Practical, not abstract.

You leave each session with something to try, not just something to think about. Sessions are structured around what you came in with, not where the conversation drifts.

ii.

In your language.

Sessions in English, Hindi, or Punjabi. Not a polite courtesy add-on. The way you describe what’s wrong matters, and it’s often easier in the language you grew up arguing in.

iii.

Built for men who don’t want a feelings circle.

Most of the men I work with took a year or two to walk through this door. They want tools, structure, and someone who isn’t going to wait them out in silence. That’s the work.

iv.

Direct billing where it matters.

WSIB direct billing for motor vehicle and workplace claims. Most major insurance providers reimburse psychotherapy. Receipts handled the same day.

03 — About Sim

A short, honest introduction.

I’m Sim. I work with adults across the GTA, mostly South Asian men dealing with anxiety, work stress, ADHD, relationship strain, and recovery after motor vehicle accidents.

I trained in solution-focused, DBT, and trauma-informed CBT approaches. In session that translates to: we figure out what’s actually getting in your way, and we work on it. No long preambles, no jargon.

Outside of clinical training, I grew up in a South Asian household. I know what log kya kahenge sounds like at the dinner table. I know what it costs to be the one everyone leans on. That context shows up in how I work.

Credentials & Supervision

Registered Psychotherapist (Qualifying) and Canadian Certified Counsellor, under the clinical supervision of Skerrett Psychology. Every session reflects two clinicians’ thinking — mine, and my supervisor’s. It’s a feature, not a footnote.

05 — Fees & FAQ

The practical stuff, said plainly.

How much is a session?
$195 for a 50-minute individual session. Most major insurance providers reimburse psychotherapy. Sliding scale availability is determined through Skerrett Psychology — ask if cost is a barrier.
Do you bill WSIB directly?
Yes. Direct billing for WSIB and motor vehicle accident claims. Same-day receipts for everyone else.
What if I’m not sure I’m “bad enough” to need therapy?
That’s the single most common thing I hear in a first session. You don’t need to be in crisis to do this work. If anything, starting earlier is easier.
In person or virtual?
Both. Virtual sessions across Ontario. In-person sessions through Skerrett Psychology in Mississauga.
Why does the booking link say Skerrett Psychology?
Because that’s where I practice, under clinical supervision. Two clinicians’ thinking on every case — not a workaround, an upgrade.

You don’t have to have it figured out before you book.

That’s the work. Most people start with the conversation, not the calendar.
